The ceramic coating itself is not where most failed coatings go wrong. The failure point is almost always the step immediately before application – specifically, whether the panel was properly wiped with isopropyl alcohol before the coating touched the surface.

Polish oils are the primary culprit. Any machine polishing pass, no matter how thoroughly buffed out, leaves behind carrier oil residue from the compound or polish. That residue is invisible to the eye and imperceptible to the touch. Ceramic coatings are designed to bond directly to the clear coat at a molecular level. Interpose a layer of polish oil between the coating and the clear coat, and that bond is compromised from the first moment of application. The coating may appear to cure correctly, but adhesion will be uneven and longevity will fall short of what the product is capable of.

Wax and sealant residue behaves the same way. Trace amounts remain in panel recesses, along body lines, and in areas that the wash, clay, and polish stages did not fully reach. Ceramic coatings do not bond over wax. The IPA wipe is the last line of defense against contamination the earlier prep stages could not eliminate.

## The Correct IPA Dilution

Isopropyl alcohol for a panel wipe should be diluted to 70–90 percent concentration in distilled water. This range provides sufficient solvent action to dissolve polish oils and wax residue without stressing rubber seals, plastic trim, or fresh polishing work.

Below 70 percent, the water content is high enough to leave its own mineral residue on the surface – a real concern in the Tampa Bay area where tap water carries hard mineral content. Above 90 percent, the solvent evaporates too quickly in Florida's heat to complete a proper wipe before the alcohol is gone. In Pasco County and across North Hillsborough during summer months, ambient temperatures push panel surfaces well above air temperature, accelerating evaporation meaningfully.

Distilled water for dilution is non-negotiable. Using tap water to cut IPA introduces the mineral contamination you are working to eliminate. Pre-diluted IPA wipe solutions formulated specifically for ceramic coating prep are a reliable alternative – concentration is controlled, the solution is prepared with distilled water, and there is no measuring involved.

## Wipe Technique: One Direction, One Pass

The rule is simple: one direction, one pass per section. Work one panel at a time. Apply the IPA solution to the applicator, not the panel. Wipe in a single linear direction – top to bottom or left to right depending on panel orientation – without reversing the stroke. Flip to a fresh face before moving to the next panel.

Circular motions redistribute contamination. Reversing the stroke drags dissolved residue back across a surface you just cleaned. The point of the wipe is to lift contamination and carry it away from the panel entirely.

Panel size determines how many sections you need. A door panel is typically one section. A roof or hood should be divided into two – work half, flip to a clean face, work the other half. This prevents the applicator from becoming saturated with dissolved contamination before the panel is finished.

## Why the Towel Choice Matters

The applicator fabric is a variable that gets underestimated. You need a towel with short, dense pile that makes consistent contact with flat panel surfaces and releases contamination cleanly rather than redepositing it.

[The Roman](/go/the-roman) is a 300 GSM waffle weave towel built for exactly this application. The waffle construction provides direct surface contact – there is no long pile to drag, trap, and redeposit dissolved residue. At 300 GSM, the towel is light enough to give tactile confirmation that the wipe is in full contact with the surface, which matters when you are executing a single deliberate pass.

A high-pile plush towel is the wrong choice here. The pile traps dissolved oils in the weave and can release them back onto the panel mid-stroke. Clean, controlled passes are also harder to execute with more material dragging across the surface.

Use a freshly laundered towel for every panel wipe session. Residual wash chemicals or fabric conditioner from a previous laundry cycle can transfer to the paint and introduce exactly the contamination you are trying to eliminate.

## Timing the Wipe

The IPA panel wipe happens after the final polish stage and after polish residue removal, but immediately before coating application. The window between a completed wipe and the first coating pass should be as short as practical – under ten minutes per panel is the target.

In Florida's climate, humid air deposits trace moisture and organic particulate on freshly wiped surfaces faster than in dry climates. Anyone moving near the vehicle introduces fingerprint oils and airborne contamination at a measurable rate. Wipe a section and coat it. Do not wipe the entire vehicle and then return to the beginning.

Any panel that gets touched after the IPA wipe needs to be re-wiped before coating. A single fingerprint on a freshly prepared panel is enough contamination to create a bonding failure in the area it covers.
