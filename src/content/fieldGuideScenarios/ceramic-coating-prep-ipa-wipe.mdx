Ceramic coating fails at the preparation stage more often than it fails at the application stage. The coating itself is reliable chemistry. What it cannot overcome is a panel surface that looks clean but carries an invisible film of polish oils, wax residue, or skin oils from fingerprints. These residues sit between the coating and the clear coat and prevent the silica network from bonding to the substrate. The result is premature failure, inconsistent adhesion, or a cured surface that beads unevenly from the first week.

The IPA panel wipe is the step that eliminates that variable. Done correctly, it leaves the panel chemically bare and ready to accept the coating. Done incorrectly – wrong dilution, wrong towel, wrong pass direction – it redistributes contamination rather than removing it.

## Why Polish Oils Survive Everything Before This Step

Polishing introduces oils into the surface. This is inherent to how modern compound and polish formulations work: the carrier oils in the product reduce heat, lubricate the abrasive, and help the compound spread evenly under the machine. Once polishing is complete, those oils remain as a thin residue, often invisible to the eye and undetectable to the touch.

A standard post-polish wipe with a clean microfiber removes the bulk of dried compound residue but does not cut through the oil film. In Florida's climate, where high ambient temperatures accelerate the way oils cure onto paint, a polished panel that sits in a warm shop for even an hour will have that film fixed more firmly than it would anywhere cooler. The same heat that complicates working outdoors in Pasco County and across the Tampa Bay area makes pre-coat prep more time-sensitive than most guides acknowledge.

IPA at the correct dilution cuts oil. Water does not. The alcohol fraction dissolves the polish carrier oil and pulls it off the surface into the towel. Provided the towel handles the contamination correctly, the panel is left chemically clean.

## Dilution: The 70–90 Percent Window

The effective range for IPA panel wipe solutions is 70 to 90 percent isopropyl alcohol to distilled water. Higher concentration – closer to 90 percent – cuts oily residue faster and flashes off more quickly, which matters in humid conditions where dwell time introduces the risk of moisture contamination before the coating goes down.

In summer conditions across North Hillsborough and the wider Tampa Bay area, where shop humidity runs high even with climate control, we use the higher end of that range on final prep wipes. The fast flash-off reduces the window for airborne contamination to re-settle on a freshly wiped panel.

Do not use drug-store rubbing alcohol without checking the label. Many consumer-grade products include glycerin or added fragrance. Those additives deposit a film rather than removing one. Use automotive-grade IPA or pharmacy-grade 91 percent isopropyl diluted with distilled water to reach your target concentration.

## The One-Direction, One-Pass Rule

Cross-contamination is the most common error during panel wipes. The logic is straightforward: you are picking up contamination from the paint surface and transferring it to the towel. Any pass that reverses direction or reuses a contaminated towel face puts that material back on the panel.

The correct method is a straight pass in one direction across the panel section, using even, moderate pressure. Flip to a clean towel face. Work the next section. Do not make a return pass with the same face that just picked up polish residue.

Work one panel at a time and coat that panel within a few minutes of wiping it. Waiting between wipe and coat – particularly in a shop with air movement – introduces airborne particles and fingerprint contamination during coating setup.

## Why the Towel Is Not Secondary

A standard plush microfiber is the wrong tool for this step. High-pile towels have less consistent flat contact with the paint surface, which reduces mechanical lift on thin oil films. The IPA does the chemical work, but the towel delivers the mechanical wipe that physically removes the dissolved material.

## What to Look For After the Wipe

After the final IPA wipe, inspect the panel under a bright light held at a low angle. The surface should show an optically uniform reflection with no variation in sheen. Any section that looks hazy or slightly different has residue remaining – most often at panel edges or in areas with complex curvature where towel contact was inconsistent.

Repeat the wipe on any such section with fresh IPA on a clean towel face. After passing the visual check, do not touch the panel. Position your coating application so you are not reaching across a completed section.
