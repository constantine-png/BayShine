Every ceramic coating failure we examine has a common thread. The coating itself is rarely the problem. The prep is – specifically, the final surface wipe before the coating goes on. That step is most often rushed, done with the wrong dilution, and done with whatever shop towel is closest.

This matters because ceramic coatings cure by forming a chemical bond with the clear coat surface. Any contamination sitting between the coating and the clear coat – polish oils, wax residue, fingerprints, silicone from prior dressings – acts as a release layer. The coating cannot bond through it. The result is a coating that may look fine for the first month, then delaminate in patches as Florida's UV load and heat cycling stress the surface.

The IPA panel wipe does not improve outcomes. It determines whether bonding occurs at all.

## What You Are Actually Removing

After paint correction, the surface looks clean under lights. It is not clean in terms of chemistry.

Polishing compounds leave behind carrier oils and lubricant residue from the compound and the pad. Even if you wiped the surface after polishing, oils that have spread into the microscopic texture of the clear coat require a solvent to lift them. Water and a microfiber alone do not accomplish that.

Wax and sealant residue from previous protection layers presents the same problem. Silicone and synthetic polymer chains from old sealants are exactly what IPA is designed to cut. In Pasco County and North Hillsborough vehicles with years of wax and spray sealant buildup, this layer can be significant.

Fingerprints are the third category, and the most overlooked. During correction, masking removal, and panel inspection, every skin contact deposits sebaceous oil onto the surface. Any panel touched after the IPA wipe needs to be wiped again before coating application.

## The Correct Dilution

Isopropyl alcohol for panel wipe should be diluted to 70 to 90 percent with distilled water. Most coating manufacturers specify this range. Below 70 percent, the water-to-solvent ratio is too high for consistent oil removal. Above 90 percent, the evaporation rate becomes a problem in warm climates – in a Florida summer, a 99 percent IPA solution can flash before the towel finishes the pass, leaving dissolved contamination partially redistributed rather than fully removed.

In Tampa Bay heat, 70 to 80 percent is the practical working range. The slightly higher water content slows evaporation just enough to allow a clean wipe-off before the solution dries on the surface.

Use distilled water, not tap. Pasco County tap water carries dissolved minerals that leave deposits on a freshly prepped surface and then get locked under the coating.

## Wipe Technique: One Direction, One Pass

Fold a clean [The Roman](/go/the-roman) into quarters. The waffle weave construction provides consistent contact with a flat panel surface without dragging, and the 300 GSM weight means the towel holds what it picks up rather than redistributing it. Apply IPA to the towel, not the panel – a few sprays per folded face, damp but not saturated.

Wipe in one direction: straight passes, not circular. Circular motions redistribute contamination back over areas you have already cleaned. Straight passes from one edge to the other move contamination off the panel face and into the towel. One pass per folded face. When that face is loaded, rotate to a clean section. Do not return to a face you have already used.

Work one panel at a time. Wipe a panel, inspect under a panel light or flashlight held at a low angle, then apply the coating before moving to the next panel. If you wipe the entire vehicle and then go back to coat, the first panels collect airborne contamination and fingerprints while you work through the rest of the car.

## The Towel Variable

The wipe towel is not a secondary consideration. A towel that leaves lint on a prepped surface deposits contamination directly under the coating. A towel that redistributes rather than lifts defeats the purpose of the step entirely.

High-GSM plush towels that work well for sealant removal are wrong for panel wipe. They carry too much material in the pile and surface contact is inconsistent. Short-pile, waffle weave towels in the 300 GSM range are the correct category – low drag, consistent contact, strong lift capacity for oil-based contamination.

## After the Wipe

Inspect every wiped panel under direct light before the coating goes on. A residue-free surface shows clean, even reflections with no greasy bloom or smear pattern. If a panel still shows a faint film after one wipe pass, run a second pass with a fresh towel section. Two clean passes are better than one pass repeated on the same face.

From this point, nothing touches the surface except the coating applicator block. Gloves on. Any skin contact with an already-wiped panel means re-wiping that section before proceeding.

The IPA wipe is the last quality control checkpoint before the coating locks in. Skipping it – or doing it poorly – is the most consistent reason a coating underperforms its rated lifespan.
