Every coating failure has a cause. When a ceramic coating delaminates early, fisheyes on application, or bonds unevenly across panels, the failure was introduced before the coating touched the paint. The IPA panel wipe is the final gatekeeping step between surface prep and coating application, and treating it as a formality is the most common way an otherwise correct coating job goes wrong.

## What the Panel Wipe Is Actually Removing

After paint correction, the surface looks clean. Under magnification, it is not. The polishing process introduces carrier oils into the micro-texture of the clear coat. These oils help the compound and polish work correctly during correction, but they are exactly what ceramic chemistry cannot bond through. Wax residue compounds the problem. Add fingerprints from handling panels during inspection and you have a surface that looks bare to the eye but is chemically contaminated.

Ceramic coatings bond through a condensation cure process – the coating's active chemistry reacts with hydroxyl groups on the clear coat surface. Polish oil, wax film, and silicone-based residue sit on top of those hydroxyl sites and block the bond. The IPA wipe's job is to remove those contaminants without introducing anything new.

This is also why the panel wipe happens after polish removal and before coating application, with no steps in between. Touching a wiped panel with bare hands, or waiting long enough for airborne contamination to settle, requires re-wiping before coating proceeds.

## IPA Dilution: Why 70 to 90 Percent Is the Working Range

Isopropyl alcohol at full concentration evaporates so quickly in warm conditions that it clears the surface before it has finished working. In Florida's climate, where temperatures in Pasco County and across the Tampa Bay area routinely run above 85 degrees during detailing season, this is not a theoretical problem. The product flashes off before contamination has been fully lifted into the solvent.

A dilution in the 70 to 90 percent range slows evaporation just enough that the IPA maintains surface contact for a complete wipe pass. Mix by volume using distilled water. Tap water introduces minerals that re-contaminate the surface you are preparing.

At dilutions below 70 percent, cleaning power drops enough to matter. The solution leaves oils mobilized but not fully removed, which can spread contamination across a larger surface area rather than eliminating it.

## Wipe Technique: One Direction, One Pass

Technique matters as much as dilution. The error most often seen in DIY ceramic prep is folding the same towel face over contaminated sections, or using circular motions that redistribute what the IPA has dissolved.

The correct method: fold a clean towel into quarters to give yourself a clean working face. Wipe in one direction – straight passes across the panel, following the vehicle's length. When the face loads with contamination, refold to a fresh face. Do not reverse the stroke. One pass per panel section, fresh towel face each time.

[The Roman](/go/the-roman) is the correct towel for this step. At 300 GSM with a waffle weave construction, it holds dissolved contamination in the weave rather than dragging it back across the surface. The lower pile height also reduces lint deposit risk on freshly wiped clear coat. Plush, high-GSM drying towels are the wrong tool here – the pile holds and re-deposits exactly what you are trying to remove.

Work one panel at a time. Wipe, inspect against direct light, then coat before moving on. In Florida's humidity, leaving wiped panels exposed for more than a few minutes allows airborne moisture and contamination to settle. The sequence is: wipe, inspect, coat, move on.

## Reading the Wipe for Surface Condition

The IPA wipe is diagnostic as well as preparatory. A towel face that pulls visible yellow or tan residue indicates polish oil contamination. Gray or dark residue points to embedded fallout that was not fully addressed in the decontamination stage.

A clean towel face after the first pass confirms the surface is ready. If the towel continues to pull contamination on a second pass, the prep work upstream was incomplete. More IPA passes are not the solution – re-evaluate the decontamination and correction sequence. That problem gets resolved before the wipe stage, not during it.

## Temperature and Environment

In the Tampa Bay area and across North Hillsborough, coating application above 80 degrees surface temperature shortens the working window between wipe and coat significantly. A panel that reaches 90 degrees will flash IPA faster, reducing effective contact time. Work in a shaded or enclosed space where possible, with ambient temperatures between 60 and 80 degrees.

If the surface is too hot to hold a palm flat against the panel for five seconds, let it cool before wiping. In Florida's summer months, this is not optional.

## What Gets Missed When This Step Is Skipped

The IPA panel wipe takes roughly thirty seconds per panel. The three variables that determine whether it works are: correct dilution, a low-GSM waffle weave towel, and single-direction passes with a fresh towel face every time. Skip any one and the step provides false confidence rather than real surface preparation – and a ceramic coating applied over residual contamination will show that failure within the first season.
